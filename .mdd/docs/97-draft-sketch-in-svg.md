# 97 — Draft Sketch in SVG

## Purpose

Feature 2 of Wave 24, the first of stage 2. Warren Davison's Draft Sketch is an
ArcGIS Pro style; the plan is SVG. This converts his style into a theme the plan
can be drawn in — a derivation from his file, regenerated by a script, never
handwork — and names what does not survive the crossing. It is seen only on the
preview, and only when asked for, until he has seen it (the wave's courtesy).

## What the style is made of (read 2026-09-18)

`Draft_Sketch.stylx`, 17,096,704 bytes, SHA-256 `4ef38ef8…668ac6`: a SQLite
database, CIM 2.3.0, 97 symbols in `ITEMS` (14 point, 35 line, 48 polygon), each
a JSON document ending in a NUL, with 46 distinct images (0.7 MB) embedded again
and again. It is mostly scanned ink and paint:

| CIM layer | Count | What it is in his style |
|---|---|---|
| `CIMPictureMarker` | 173 | tinted splotches strewn inside a polygon, grass tufts, tree centres, marks on corners and along edges |
| `CIMPictureStroke` | 93 | the ink line: a scanned strip drawn along the outline |
| `CIMPictureFill` | 38 | a tinted paper texture |
| `CIMSolidFill` / `CIMSolidStroke` | 72 / 52 | the base wash; plain lines |
| `CIMGradientFill` | 37 | crowns (linear, circular) and "buffered" soft inner edges |
| `CIMHatchFill` / `CIMVectorMarker` | 10 / 22 | brick grids, sand grains |
| effects | 204 Wave, 44 Buffer, 22 Move, 15 Offset, 12 Dashes, … | the wobble, drop shadows |

## Architecture

```
assets/draft-sketch/source.json ── url, bytes, sha256 (the .stylx itself is not committed)
            │  python -m scripts.stylx_to_theme [--fetch] [--check]
            ▼
scripts/draft_sketch/png.py      read his PNGs — standard library only
scripts/draft_sketch/cim.py      read the chosen symbols' layers
scripts/draft_sketch/tiles.py    a symbol's fill as one repeating tile
scripts/draft_sketch/outline.py  what a symbol draws along a shape, as overlays
scripts/draft_sketch/emit.py     write both
            ▼
frontend/src/themes/draft-sketch/generated/
    images/ds-<sha8>.png  his images, unchanged, only those drawn     (his)
    symbols.ts            masks, ramps and patterns, as SVG markup     Provenance: Warren Davison (Draft Sketch)
    rules.ts              overlays per symbol, in metres               Provenance: adapted from Draft Sketch
frontend/src/themes/draft-sketch/index.ts   the theme: our symbols → his, per level of detail
frontend/src/themes/draft-sketch/Defs.tsx   his markup, parsed by the browser in one go
frontend/src/themes/draft-sketch/draw.tsx   overlays → SVG, from each shape's own outline
frontend/src/canvas/sketch.ts               wobble, overshoots, ticks, offset — pure geometry
frontend/src/components/PlanDecorations.tsx shadows under, ink over the shapes; never a target
frontend/src/components/PlanCredit.tsx      his credit on the plan, whenever it is his style
```

**Two halves, because SVG has two halves.** What fills a shape — base wash,
tinted paper, strewn splotches, tufts, grains, brick grid — becomes a
`<pattern>` once, generated. What follows a shape's outline — the ink, its
wobble, the rim, the corners, the ticks, the drop shadow, a tree's ramps and
centre — is drawn per shape from its footprint, from `rules.ts`. The shape
itself stays the exact polygon it always was, the only hit target.

**His images ship once, and are tinted in SVG.** Pre-tinting each image per
symbol would have shipped 34 copies, 2.2 MB, most of it one 274 KB paper
texture in eight colours; shipping each once is 8 images, 344 KB. A tint is
drawn as his colour through the image's alpha (`<mask mask-type="alpha">`).

**The theme is its own chunk.** `loadTheme` imports it only when it is drawn,
so production never fetches it. Vite puts the chunk and his images under
`assets/draft-sketch/`, and the server answers 404 there anywhere but on the
preview (`ninanatur/web/delivery.py`): a release that carried the code before
his look would still not serve his files.

## Units and levels of detail

CIM measures in points at print scale; the plan in metres. One reference scale
converts them — **1:250, so a point is 0.0882 m**. Ink is never drawn thinner
than a pixel. His three levels of detail are for 0–1:1,500, 1:1,500–2,500 and
beyond; at 96 dpi that is 0.40 and 0.66 metres per pixel, which `lodAt` uses.

## What is converted (our symbol → his)

| Our symbol (kinds) | His symbol | Level of detail |
|---|---|---|
| building (house, shed) | Buildings (LOD 1), Building (LOD 2), Building (LOD 3) | three |
| crown (tree, shrub) | Tree 1 (LOD 1/2/3), polygon | three |
| grass (lawn) | Grass | one |
| water (pond) | Water (area) | one |
| stipple (gravel) | Sand | one |
| slabs (paving, path) | Interlock Brick Grid | one |
| tarmac (street) | Gray Fill | one |
| planting (bed) | Brown Fill | one |
| foliage (hedge), masonry (wall), fence, plain (other) | Dark Green, Gray, Brown and Gray Fill | one — feature 3 draws them properly |
| plain (the garden's ground) | Dashed Outline, no fill | one |

His heavy outline round the hedges, walls and fences was tried and turned a
fence into a black bar; they carry their wash's own ink until feature 3.

## Read off his own style sheet

What CIM does not say, his published style sheet shows — the ground truth for
what his symbols look like drawn (viewed 2026-09-18):

- **A tint multiplies.** His trees' "white-tinted" strokes are black: black ink
  stays black whatever it is tinted, a white wash takes the tint's colour. So
  ink is drawn as it is and a wash through a mask; an image that is neither is
  refused by the converter.
- **A linear ramp starts where its angle points**: his trees are lit from the
  upper left, opposite their shadows. **A circular ramp's first colour is its
  rim**: his crowns are solid in the middle.
- **His buildings carry ticks** along their inner edge, and their edges run on
  past their corners — both drawn as the short strokes they are.

## What does not survive, named

- **The ink strip along a line** (`CIMPictureStroke`): SVG cannot lay an image
  along a path. Ink becomes a solid line as wide as the ink in the strip (58 %
  of it); a rim — a stroke moved inside the shape — keeps its width and becomes
  as faint as its strip (24 %).
- **"Buffered" ramps** — the grey inside a building's edge, water's darker rim:
  SVG has no distance-to-edge paint. Their inside colour is the base; the rim
  is left for feature 4.
- **The bleed**: his splotches are strewn inside and spill over the outline;
  ours are a pattern clipped to it. Feature 4.
- **The wobble of a fill's edge**: the fill is the exact polygon (the hit
  target); the wobbled ink over it covers the difference.
- **A gap cut in an outline** (`Cut`), **"Random" waveforms** (seeded here: the
  same line on every render), **print-scale truths** (text boxes, callouts).

## Business Rules

1. **A derivation, not handwork.** `python -m scripts.stylx_to_theme --check`
   regenerates into a temporary folder and compares byte for byte with what is
   committed; it exits 1 on any difference, and so does the test. The .stylx
   must match `source.json`'s size and SHA-256 before anything is read.
2. **Nothing downloads by itself.** Without the file the converter says where
   it comes from and stops; `--fetch` downloads it, and only then.
3. **No `data:` URIs.** The CSP allows images from the site itself only:
   `assetsInlineLimit: 0`, and a test checks every image is a file.
4. **Provenance.** `symbols.ts` names *Warren Davison (Draft Sketch)*,
   `rules.ts` *adapted from Draft Sketch*; the provenance test covers a theme's
   subfolders and its rules files. `THIRD_PARTY.md` records the permission.
5. **Nothing of his is public before he has seen it.** Drawn only on the
   preview (`environment` = `dev`), only by `?theme=draft-sketch`; a chunk of
   its own that production never asks for, and would not serve if asked.
6. **His credit goes with his style**, word for word as agreed, on the plan
   whenever it is drawn in it.
7. **Technisch is untouched**: doc 95's record passes, all 24 cells.
8. **Everything his markup refers to is in it** — a tinted mark whose mask is
   missing is a hard square of colour. Tested on the markup and on the plan.
9. **The budget.** The city at 40 m, CPU ×4, against Technisch's recorded
   36 ms: twice that is the limit; beyond it is a finding for stage 3.

## The paint budget, measured

The city block (102 elements) at 40 m with the CPU slowed four times, median of
five: **69 ms** against Technisch's 36 ms (recorded, and measured again the same
day) — 1.9 times, within the limit of 72 with little to spare. The measure
waits for two frames, so it moves in steps of about 16 ms: Technisch's five runs
were 53, 36, 22, 21 and 36; Draft Sketch's 121, 72, 42, 68 and 69.

How it got there: Chromium records a pattern's content again for every shape
it fills. With his marks in the pattern itself the city took **221 ms**. Now
each pattern is one rect filled with the drawn tile, which has a single user
and is recorded once; dense layers (grains, a crown's splotches, ripples) sit
on small sheets of a dozen marks; a wobble that would not move a line by a
pixel is drawn straight; a shape's decorations get a group only while dragged.
The city's 1,681 nodes became 1,298 (705 in his defs, 336 decorations;
Technisch draws 304).

## Dark mode

His style is black ink on white paper. In dark mode the plan stays paper — a
sheet on a dark desk, with the light palette's marks on it — rather than his
ink turned white, which would be another style (`theme.css`). Feature 4 gives
the paper its grain.

## Dependencies

- 96 — the seam; this adds three optional members: `decorate`, `images`,
  `credit`.
- 95 — the sheet it is judged on: `--theme draft-sketch` draws it, with 200 m
  and 400 m columns for the other levels of detail, and is never recorded.

## Security

The converter reads a file this project does not make: it is refused unless its
size and SHA-256 match the pin, opened read-only, and parsed as JSON — no code
in it is executed; embedded images must be PNG, decoded by the standard
library. The generated markup is set with `innerHTML` (`Defs.tsx`): it is built
from ids, numbers, colours and path data that `emit.py` checks against strict
patterns and refuses otherwise, plus this bundle's own image URLs — nothing a
user or his file's text can put there. The CSP is unchanged (`style-src 'self'`:
the markup uses presentation attributes, never `style=""`).
